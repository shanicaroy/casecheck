The tool checks each dimension in order. Each check returns: present / weak / missing, one piece of evidence from the portfolio, and a confidence level.

**A. Problem framing ★**
Does the case study start from a real problem, in the designer's own words, or from a template stage ("Empathise")? Branches by project type:
- *Revamp:* is it clear what existed before, what was wrong with it, and what it is now? A before/after that a reader can actually see.
- *Zero-to-one:* is there an initial state of ambiguity, and can the reader follow how the designer moved from that ambiguity to a clear, defined problem statement?
Fail signals: the problem appears fully formed with no origin; the "before" is never shown; the problem statement is a feature request in disguise.

**B. Thinking, not process ★**
Does the reader see *how* the designer thought about solving the problem, or only *which stages* they went through? A framework (design thinking, double diamond) is acceptable, especially for very junior designers who need scaffolding, but the reasoning underneath the framework must be visible. The test: could you remove the stage labels and still follow the logic?
Fail signals: stage headers with generic content beneath; every project on the portfolio follows the identical shape.

**C. Research authenticity ★**
Do the research artefacts exist because they produced an insight that changed something? Personas, journey maps, interviews: each must be traceable to a decision it influenced. If a persona exists, the case study should show what the designer learned from it that they didn't know before.
Fail signals: persona present, never referenced again; research section with no insight stated; artefacts that read as AI-generated filler (generic demographics, invented quotes, stock pain points).

**D. Constraints and realism ★**
Were there real constraints (time, technical, business, stakeholders, budget, legacy systems) and does the case study name them? Was there pushback, a challenge, something that didn't go to plan? A frictionless story is a red flag, not a strength.
Fail signals: no constraint named anywhere; the process reads as smooth from brief to launch; no stakeholder or engineer ever disagreed.

**E. Iteration ★**
Is there visible iteration, and does the case study show *what changed between versions and why*? Number of iterations matters less than whether the reader can see the reasoning that drove the change.
Fail signals: one version shown as final; "we iterated" stated without evidence; iterations shown as a gallery with no rationale.

**F. Decisions and tradeoffs**
Are the key decisions named as decisions, with the alternative that was rejected and the reason? A senior reader is looking for judgment: "we chose X over Y because Z."
Fail signals: the solution simply appears; no alternative is ever mentioned; decisions attributed to "the team" with no personal stance.

**G. Role clarity**
Is it clear what *this* designer did, versus the team? Especially for group or agency projects.
Fail signals: "we" throughout with no "I"; ownership implied but never stated; role listed as "UX/UI designer" with no specifics.

**H. Outcome and honesty ★**
Is there a stated result, and is it honest? A real metric, a qualitative outcome, a shipped state, or a clear statement that the outcome isn't known yet (student project, unshipped) are all acceptable. Inflated or unverifiable claims are not.
Fail signals: no outcome at all; suspiciously round success metrics with no source; "increased engagement by 300%" with nothing behind it.

**I. Learnings and insights**
What did the designer learn from this project, and what would they do differently? This should be specific to the project, not a general reflection.
Fail signals: no learnings section; learnings that could apply to any project ("I learned the value of user research").

**J. Editing and readability**
Can a hiring manager get the story in two minutes? Is the case study edited down to what matters, or is it a 40-screen scroll of every artefact produced?
Fail signals: no hierarchy; every deliverable shown at equal weight; the key decision is buried two-thirds of the way down.

**K. AI-generation signals (cross-cutting)**
Not a dimension on its own, but a flag raised on any dimension where the content reads as generated rather than lived: generic personas, invented user quotes, stock insights, uniform paragraph rhythm, claims with no specific detail behind them. Raised as a low-to-medium confidence flag with the specific evidence, never as an accusation.

**L. Craft — visual and execution** *(see scope note)*
Is the design polished: clean UI, sense of colour, typographic taste, consistent execution, restraint? Minimal and precise beats decorated.
